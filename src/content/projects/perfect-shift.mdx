Perfect Shift is a small arcade game about one thing: changing gear at exactly
the right moment. I designed and built it alone, as a personal project.

It's styled like a car's dashboard: dark, minimal, a large tachometer in the
middle, a few accent colours for the zones. That's deliberate. The idea is a
proof of concept for something that could run on a car's infotainment screen
while it's parked or charging, using the instrument everyone already reads.

## How it plays

A run starts with launch control, then the car climbs through five gears. The
needle doesn't climb once per gear the way it would in a real car; it swings
back and forth like a pendulum, and you tap when it's in the green. Perfect
shifts close most of the gap to your top speed, sloppy ones cost you. Corners
force a quick downshift. In Solo Survival the needle speeds up over time and
three mistakes end the run; in Beat the AI you race a rival that also misses
corners, more often at the easier levels.

## How it's built

It's Unity 6 with C#, around fifty scripts kept small and separate: the
gearbox, the RPM model, shift evaluation, scoring, difficulty, corner events
and the AI rival each live on their own. That separation is what made it
testable. The game logic is covered by 110 edit-mode tests, so tuning a number
like the size of the green zone doesn't quietly break the scoring.
