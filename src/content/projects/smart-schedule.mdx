Smart Schedule started as a team project at Babeș-Bolyai University. The
faculty publishes its timetables as a set of HTML tables, one per group, and
every student ends up copying their own into a calendar by hand, then checking
back in case something moved.

We built an app that does that for them. You choose your group, add your
optional subjects, and subscribe once. The schedule appears in whatever calendar
app you already use, and it keeps itself up to date.

## What I built

I led the backend. The core of it is mine: the data model for timetables,
groups and teachers, the scrapers and parsers that read the faculty's HTML, the
cache that keeps the app fast without hammering the faculty's server, and moving
the whole server into shape for deployment. Later I fixed the parts of a
calendar that are easy to get wrong: odd and even teaching weeks, final-year
students whose semester ends early, and the holidays, all with tests.

The Flutter app, the notifications and the CI pipelines came from teammates.
Eight of us contributed in total.

## How it works

The backend is Node.js and TypeScript on Express. It scrapes the faculty's
timetable pages and academic calendar, parses them into structured data, and
caches the result, with an endpoint to refresh it on demand. Each student's
timetable is stored in MongoDB and rendered into a personal iCalendar feed that
calendar apps subscribe to. A scheduled job runs every morning at six, diffs the
fresh timetables against what students saved, and updates whatever changed.

## Where it stands

The backend runs in production and students use it, and the faculty's
Vice-Dean recognised the project. I still contribute, and it has been handed on
to younger students at the faculty to keep it going.
